MG Profile 01

Founder & Head Coach

Mauricio Gomez

Judo — 4th Dan Blackbelt Jiu-Jitsu — 3rd Dan Blackbelt Karate — 1st Dan Blackbelt Accredited Muay Thai coach

Mauricio founded DoBu Martial Arts and still runs it day to day — from coaching the 06:00 Jiu-Jitsu class to overseeing the gym floor, the timetable and the grading syllabus.

He holds black belts in three separate martial arts, which makes him the only coach at DoBu qualified to teach across every discipline on the mat. His own training began with Judo as a child, and the throwing art remains the foundation of how he teaches.

He's a strong believer that grappling and striking should inform each other — a Judo throw teaches you how to close distance, which makes your Karate or Muay Thai entries sharper; a Muay Thai clinch teaches you grip fighting, which feeds straight back into Judo.

As head coach, Mauricio leads the Judo and Jiu-Jitsu programmes alongside Guy Victory, and teaches the Muay Thai classes alongside Morris Davis. He also handles belt gradings across all disciplines, and is the person to talk to if you're not sure which art to start with.

His teaching style is technical and patient. He'll break a throw down into its component parts, drill each one, then build them back up — and he'd rather you do a technique slowly and correctly a hundred times than fast and sloppy ten times.

SN Profile 02

Karate Programme Lead

Sarah Nova

Karate — 5th Dan Blackbelt Highest-graded coach at DoBu

Sarah holds a 5th Dan in Karate — the highest grade of any coach at DoBu — and leads the entire Karate programme, from the six-year-olds in the Wednesday afternoon juniors class to senior adults training for their next Dan grade.

She came to Karate through competition, and spent over a decade on the tournament circuit before moving into coaching. That competitive background shapes her teaching: she places a strong emphasis on distance, timing and decisive technique — the things that actually win a point, rather than the things that look good in practice.

Her classes follow the traditional three pillars — kihon, kata and kumite — but she's rigorous about connecting them. Every kata movement gets its bunkai application broken out and drilled with a partner, so students understand why the form looks the way it does, not just how to perform it.

Sarah is particularly good with beginners and children. Her kids' Karate class is one of the gym's most popular sessions, and she's known for building focus and confidence in students who've never done any martial art before.

GV Profile 03

Jiu-Jitsu & Judo Coach

Guy Victory

Jiu-Jitsu — 2nd Dan Blackbelt Judo — 1st Dan Blackbelt

Guy teaches both grappling arts at DoBu, and holds black belts in each — 2nd Dan in Jiu-Jitsu and 1st Dan in Judo.

That combination makes him particularly valuable to students training across both disciplines, because he understands exactly where the two arts overlap and where they diverge. His own game is built around ground control and transitions.

He's less interested in spectacular throws than in what happens immediately afterwards — how you land in a dominant position, how you keep it, and how you move from there to a finish. That focus shows in his classes: plenty of time spent on positional drilling, escapes and the small details that make a technique work against a resisting opponent.

Guy is the coach most likely to stop a class mid-drill to correct a grip or a hip position. He's meticulous about fundamentals, and he'd rather a student spend three months getting the basics genuinely solid than rush ahead to submissions they can't safely apply.

He works closely with Mauricio on the Judo and Jiu-Jitsu syllabus, and covers the 06:00 and 19:00 grappling classes alongside him. If you're new to either art, Guy is often the coach who'll take you through your first breakfalls or your first guard escape.

MD Profile 04

Muay Thai & Karate Coach

Morris Davis

Accredited Muay Thai coach Karate — 3rd Dan Blackbelt

Morris is DoBu's striking specialist. He holds an accredited Muay Thai coaching qualification and a 3rd Dan in Karate, and teaches both arts across the adult timetable.

He came to Muay Thai from a Karate background, which gives him an unusual perspective on striking. Where a lot of Thai boxing coaching focuses purely on power and conditioning, Morris places equal weight on technique, accuracy and defence — the things that keep you safe in a real exchange.

His pad rounds are precise: he'll call combinations and correct your form mid-round, rather than just holding pads and letting you tire yourself out. In Karate, he focuses on kumite — the sparring side of the art. His classes are built around distance management, timing and controlled contact, and he's careful to introduce sparring gradually so that beginners are never thrown in before they're ready.

Morris also coaches the gym's conditioning circuits, and his Muay Thai classes are widely regarded as the hardest workout on the timetable — a reputation he takes some pride in.

07 Qualifications

What the grades
mean.

Every coach at DoBu holds a recognised qualification in the art they teach. Here's how to read those numbers.

Dan grades

Black belt and above

Dan grades are the senior ranks in Japanese martial arts, starting at 1st Dan. Progression is by examination and takes years per grade — a 5th Dan typically represents two to three decades of continuous training and teaching.

Kyu grades

Coloured belts

Kyu grades are the ranks below black belt, working downward in number as you progress. Students at DoBu grade twice a year, assessed in class by a senior coach against a published syllabus.

Accreditation

Coaching qualifications

Muay Thai and fitness coaching are covered by accredited qualifications rather than belt grades — nationally recognised certifications covering technique, safety, coaching practice and first aid.

10 Questions

About the
coaches.

Common questions about who teaches what, how gradings work, and how to get the most out of your coaching.

01 Can I choose which coach teaches me?

For group classes, no — the coach is whoever leads that session on the timetable. That said, all our coaches teach to the same syllabus, so you'll get consistent instruction whichever session you attend.

For private tuition, yes. You can book any of the four martial arts coaches, and you'll choose who you want when you book. If you're not sure, reception can recommend the best coach for what you're working on.

02 Do all coaches teach beginners?

Yes. Every martial arts coach at DoBu teaches across all levels, including absolute beginners. There's no "beginners are handled by the junior coaches" arrangement — Mauricio, Sarah, Guy and Morris all take first-time students.

Beginners are usually paired with an experienced student during sparring, and coaches will adapt the session content depending on who's in the room.

03 Who handles belt gradings?
  • Judo & Jiu-Jitsu — Mauricio Gomez leads gradings, with Guy Victory assessing
  • Karate — Sarah Nova leads gradings, with Morris Davis and Mauricio assessing
  • Muay Thai — no grading system; progress is tracked by coach assessment

Gradings happen twice a year. Your coach will tell you well in advance when you're ready, and what's on the syllabus for your next grade.

04 How often do coaches change?

Rarely. All six coaches are long-standing members of the DoBu team, and the timetable is stable week to week. Occasional swaps happen during holidays or grading weeks, and any changes are posted on the community page in advance.

05 Do the coaches compete?

Some do, and some have a competition background they've since stepped back from. Sarah came from a decade on the Karate tournament circuit, and Mauricio has competed in Judo and Jiu-Jitsu. Morris has Muay Thai fight experience.

None of them compete regularly at present — their focus is on coaching — but the experience informs how they teach competition preparation.

06 Can I book a coach for a specific technique?

Yes, and it's one of the best uses of private tuition. If there's a throw, submission, kata or combination that isn't clicking, book an hour with the coach who teaches that art and they'll break it down with you one-to-one.

Mention what you want to work on when you book, so the coach can plan the session.

07 Is there a women-only class or coach?

We don't run women-only classes, but all our group sessions are mixed and every coach is experienced at teaching students of all backgrounds. Sarah Nova leads the Karate programme and teaches across multiple sessions if you'd prefer to start with a female coach.

If you're nervous about your first class, mention it when you book and we'll pair you with a coach and training partner who'll make it comfortable.

08 What qualifications do the fitness coaches hold?

Both fitness coaches hold sports science degrees and nationally recognised personal training qualifications:

  • Traci Santiago — BSc Sports Science, qualified in health and nutrition
  • Harpreet Kaur — BSc Physiotherapy, MSc Sports Science

Both specialise in working with combat athletes, and can build programmes around the specific demands of the martial art you train.

09 How do I give feedback about a coach?

Speak to the coach directly, or message us through the contact page. Mauricio runs the gym and reviews all feedback personally. If something's working well, we'd also like to hear that — it helps us keep the coaching team strong.

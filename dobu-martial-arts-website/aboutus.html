01 Our story

Ten years on
the same street.

DoBu opened in 2016 in a rented back room off Foundry Lane — a single roll of matting, a set of borrowed pads, and Mauricio Gomez teaching two Judo classes a week to whoever turned up. There was no reception, no changing rooms, and no fitness equipment.

What there was from the first week was a culture. Mauricio had spent years training in clubs where beginners were tolerated rather than welcomed, and he wanted the opposite. Every new student was paired with someone experienced. Every question got answered. Nobody was made to feel like they had to earn the right to be in the room.

That approach spread by word of mouth. Within two years the back room couldn't hold the classes. In 2019 the gym took over the full ground floor of the foundry building, laid a proper sprung mat, and added the fitness room. Jiu-Jitsu and Karate followed, taught by coaches Mauricio had trained with for years — Guy Victory and Sarah Nova. Muay Thai came later, when Morris Davis joined.

Today DoBu is one floor: a matted training area for four martial arts, a fully-equipped gym, sauna, steam room and changing facilities. Around 180 members train here each month, from complete beginners to competitive players, aged six to sixty-something. What hasn't changed is the reason people stay — the culture that started in a back room with a kettle.

02 How we got here

A decade,
briefly.

The milestones that took DoBu from a back-room Judo club to a four-discipline gym with its own fitness floor — in roughly chronological order.

  1. 2016 Beginning

    DoBu opens on Foundry Lane

    Mauricio Gomez starts teaching Judo twice a week in a rented back room, with a single roll of matting and a set of borrowed pads. Membership at the end of the first month: eleven people.

  2. 2018 Growth

    Jiu-Jitsu joins the timetable

    Guy Victory comes on board to teach Jiu-Jitsu, having trained with Mauricio for over a decade. The back room starts hitting capacity, and a waiting list becomes the norm for evening classes.

  3. 2019 Expansion

    The full ground floor, and a proper mat

    DoBu takes over the entire ground floor of the foundry building. A sprung competition-grade mat is laid, the first proper changing rooms are built, and Karate is added to the programme under Sarah Nova.

  4. 2021 Rebuild

    Coming back after the closures

    After a difficult period of closures, DoBu reopens with a rebuilt timetable and a focus on rebuilding the community rather than the balance sheet. Membership returns to pre-closure levels within eight months — largely on the strength of members bringing friends back.

  5. 2022 Facilities

    Fitness floor, sauna and steam room

    The old storage area is converted into a fitness room with free weights and conditioning equipment. A sauna and steam room are added alongside new changing facilities, and access is included with every membership tier from day one.

  6. 2023 Striking

    Muay Thai completes the four

    Morris Davis joins as an accredited Muay Thai coach, bringing the gym to four disciplines. With Judo, Jiu-Jitsu, Karate and Muay Thai on the same mat, members can now train grappling and striking without leaving the building.

  7. 2024 Team

    Fitness coaches join the team

    Traci Santiago and Harpreet Kaur join as dedicated fitness coaches, adding structured strength and conditioning, injury prevention and rehabilitation work to the programme — and allowing members to train for the demands of their specific art rather than a generic gym plan.

  8. Today Now

    Four arts, one floor, one culture

    Around 180 members train at DoBu each month. Thirty-one classes run across seven days. Six coaches teach, four of them holding black belts. And the founding principle hasn't moved an inch: beginners are welcomed, not tolerated.

03 What we stand for

Six things we
don't compromise on.

Not mission-statement filler. These are the principles that shape how classes are run, who gets hired, and what gets turned down.

01 / Principles

Beginners come first

Every class is designed so that someone walking in for the first time can take part fully. If a session can't be taught safely to a complete beginner, it doesn't belong on the open timetable.

02 / Culture

Technique over ego

Sparring is a learning tool, not a proving ground. Coaches will stop a round if it's becoming about winning rather than improving, and students who can't control their contact don't get to keep sparring.

03 / Access

No pressure, ever

Nobody gets chased for a membership. Trials are free without strings. Sparring is always optional. Gradings are invitations, not obligations. If you want to train quietly and go home, that's completely fine.

04 / Value

One membership, one floor

The mat, the gym, the sauna and the steam room are all included at every tier — not upsells. Training shouldn't come with a list of things you're not allowed to use.

05 / Credentials

Coaches who actually coach

Every instructor holds a graded rank or accredited qualification in what they teach. Nobody at DoBu is teaching something they haven't trained seriously for years themselves.

06 / Long-term

Community before growth

We've turned down opportunities to expand faster than the coaching team could support. More members is not the goal — more people training well, and staying, is.

05 Community

More than
a gym membership.

DoBu is a small business, but it's also a local institution — the kind of place where people meet their closest friends, get through hard years, and find something that sticks.

About a third of our members have been training here for more than three years. A handful have been here since the back-room days. That kind of retention isn't about the equipment or the timetable — those things are easy to copy. It's about the room feeling like it belongs to the people in it.

Several members have gone on to coach, grade, or compete at a level they hadn't expected when they walked in. Others have stayed exactly as they started — training twice a week, never grading, never sparring, and getting exactly what they came for.

We also do the less glamorous stuff. Junior classes for kids who need somewhere structured to be after school. Subsidised places for members going through a rough patch. A standing arrangement with two local schools to run taster sessions for students who wouldn't otherwise encounter a martial art.

None of that is marketing. It's what happens when a gym is run by people who actually train in it and plan to be here in another ten years.

07 Questions

About the gym.

Common questions about DoBu itself — how it's run, who it's for, and what you should expect when you walk in.

01 Is DoBu suitable for complete beginners?

Absolutely — it's the main reason most people come here. Every class on the open timetable is designed so someone walking in for the first time can take part fully, with no prior experience and no equipment of their own.

Beginners are paired with experienced students during sparring and drills, and coaches will adapt the session content depending on who's in the room. You'll never be thrown into anything you're not ready for.

If you'd like a fuller picture of what a first class looks like, the martial arts FAQ covers it in detail.

02 How is DoBu different from a bigger chain gym?

Three things mainly:

  • Coaches actually teach. Every instructor holds a graded rank or accredited qualification in what they're teaching, and they're all still training on the same mat as the members.
  • Nothing is upsold. The mat, the gym, the sauna and the steam room are all included at every membership tier — not just the expensive ones.
  • No pressure, no contracts. Rolling monthly memberships, cancel any time online, free first class with no card required. We'd rather people stay because they want to than because they're locked in.
03 Is DoBu a family-friendly gym?

Yes. Kids' classes run five days a week for ages six to fifteen, taught by the same coaching team that teaches the adult classes — including Sarah Nova, who leads the Karate programme.

Families with two or more junior members get 15% off the second and subsequent junior memberships. Parents are always welcome to watch from the seating area, and we run free junior trials every school term for families on the local estate.

04 How big are the classes?

Typically between eight and twenty students, depending on the slot. Early morning classes (06:00) are usually the quietest, with five to ten students. Evening classes (17:30 onwards) are the busiest, and weekday lunchtime open mat sessions sit somewhere in the middle.

We don't cap class sizes artificially, but if a session regularly gets too busy for the mat space we add another session rather than crowd the room.

05 How long has DoBu been running?

Since 2016 — over ten years on the same street in East Side. The gym started as a rented back room with one coach teaching two Judo classes a week, and has grown into a four-discipline gym with its own fitness floor, sauna and steam room.

Same building, same postcode, same name on the door. Full milestones are in the timeline above.

06 Do you have any women-only sessions?

We don't run women-only classes, but all group sessions are mixed and every coach at DoBu is experienced at teaching students of all backgrounds. Roughly a third of our members are women, and it's a normal part of the room rather than a special case.

If you'd prefer to start with a female coach, Sarah Nova leads the Karate programme and teaches across multiple sessions — mention it when you book a trial and we'll pair you accordingly.

07 Is there parking at the gym?

Yes — free on-street parking on Foundry Lane, both sides. It's busiest between 17:00 and 18:30 on weekdays, so arrive a few minutes early if you're coming to an evening class.

There's additional parking on Wharf Road about four minutes away, bicycle racks at the entrance, and East Side station is a two-minute walk. Full directions are on the contact page.

08 Can I visit before deciding on a membership?

Yes. Come and have a look around any time during gym hours — reception can show you the mat, the fitness room, the changing facilities, and answer any questions about the timetable or membership tiers.

If you'd rather just try a class, your first one is free in any discipline with no card required. Book a trial from the contact page or just turn up.

09 Is the gym accessible?

DoBu is on the ground floor of the foundry building with step-free access from Foundry Lane. There's an accessible toilet and changing facility, and a seating area next to the mat.

If you have specific access requirements, mention them in the enquiry form on the contact page or call ahead on 01632 960 482 and we'll make sure everything's ready when you arrive.

Discipline 01

Judo

Throws · Control · Groundwork  |  Adult & Kids

Beginners welcome Gi required Graded belt system Kids from age 7
Origin Japan, 1882
Focus Throws & groundwork
Session 90 minutes
Instructors Mauricio · Guy

Judo — literally "the gentle way" — is a Japanese grappling art built on a single elegant idea: rather than fighting an opponent's strength head-on, you use their own movement and momentum to take them off balance and throw them.

It's one of the most complete martial arts you can learn, because it teaches both standing and ground work in equal measure. The throwing techniques (nage-waza) develop timing, footwork and balance; the groundwork (ne-waza) teaches pins, escapes and joint locks. Between the two, you get a practical, full-range grappling system that scales to any size difference.

At DoBu, Judo is taught the traditional way: a formal bow onto the mat, a structured warm-up, then technique drilling before moving into randori — live, resisted sparring. Beginners are paired with experienced students during randori and are never thrown into full-intensity sparring before they're ready.

Because Judo involves a lot of falling, one of the very first things you'll learn is ukemi — how to breakfall safely. It's the skill that makes everything else possible, and it's drilled at the start of every single class, no matter your grade.

Discipline 02

Jiu-Jitsu

Ground control · Position · Submissions  |  Adult & Kids

Beginners welcome Gi required Graded belt system Kids from age 7
OriginJapan / Brazil
FocusGround grappling
Session90 minutes
InstructorsMauricio · Guy

Where Judo is about the throw, Jiu-Jitsu is about what happens after it. It's a ground-based grappling art built on one core principle: position before submission.

You don't chase a finish until you've secured a controlling position — and you don't escape a bad position by muscling out of it, you escape by using leverage and technique.

That's what makes it one of the most practical arts for real-world self-defence, and why it's so popular with smaller students. A well-trained Jiu-Jitsu practitioner can neutralise a much larger, stronger opponent by controlling distance, isolating limbs and applying chokes and joint locks that rely on mechanics rather than force.

Our classes follow a clear progression: escapes first, then positions, then submissions. You'll spend your early weeks learning how to survive on the bottom and get back to a safe position — because that's the skill that matters most, and everything else is built on top of it.

Sparring at DoBu is rolling: continuous, live grappling from the knees or standing, in rounds. Beginners roll with senior students and coaches for the first few months, at a controlled pace, so you're learning rather than just surviving.

Discipline 03

Karate

Striking · Kata · Discipline  |  Adult & Kids

Beginners welcome Gi required Graded belt system Kids from age 6
OriginOkinawa, Japan
FocusStriking & forms
Session90 minutes
Lead coachSarah Nova

Karate is a traditional Japanese striking art built on three interlocking pillars: kihon (basics), kata (pre-arranged forms) and kumite (sparring). Each one reinforces the others.

Kihon builds the mechanics — stance, hip rotation, precision. Kata encodes those mechanics into long, solo sequences you practise for years. Kumite tests whether they hold up against a resisting opponent.

It's often mistaken for a purely "traditional" art with little practical application, but that misses the point. Karate trains distance management, timing and decisive striking — the ability to end a confrontation with one well-placed technique, and to not be there when the counter comes. The discipline of kata practice also builds something harder to quantify: focus, patience and composure under pressure.

At DoBu, our Karate programme is led by Sarah Nova (5th Dan) and runs as a structured syllabus. New students start with the fundamentals of stance and basic strikes, then learn their first kata within the first few weeks.

Sparring is introduced gradually. Beginners start with controlled, non-contact drills, then move to light-contact point sparring once they have the control to keep it safe. Full-contact sparring is optional and only for students who want to compete.

Discipline 04

Muay Thai

Punches · Kicks · Elbows · Knees · Clinch  |  Adults

Beginners welcome Gloves required No grading system Sparring optional
OriginThailand
FocusStriking & clinch
Session90 minutes
InstructorsMauricio · Morris

Muay Thai — the "art of eight limbs" — is Thailand's national striking art, and it's called that because it uses eight points of contact rather than the two fists of boxing.

Punches, kicks, knees and elbows are all fair game, and the art is famous for its brutal efficiency and its distinctive clinch — a standing grappling range where knees and sweeps do the damage.

It's also one of the best-conditioning martial arts you can train. A Muay Thai session will push your cardio harder than almost anything else on the floor, while building genuine striking power through repeated pad work. If you want to get fit and learn something that works, this is the one.

At DoBu, classes are built around pad work and bag work rather than sparring. You'll spend the majority of every session hitting pads held by a partner or coach — which means you get real, corrective feedback on your technique from the first class, without needing to spar.

Sparring is entirely optional. Students who want to test their skills can move into controlled, light-contact sparring once they've built up the fundamentals.

05 Standalone course

Six-week beginners'
self-defence.

A structured course for complete beginners who want practical self-defence without committing to a full membership. It draws on techniques from Judo, Jiu-Jitsu and Karate — but strips them back to what actually works under pressure.

The course is built around a simple idea: most real confrontations are decided before anyone throws a punch. So the first weeks focus on awareness, distance and de-escalation, and only then move into physical technique.

  1. Week 01 Awareness & avoidanceSituational awareness, body language, recognising pre-attack indicators, and how to leave a situation before it starts.
  2. Week 02 Distance & de-escalationManaging space, verbal de-escalation, fence positioning, and the decision point between flight and fight.
  3. Week 03 Striking under pressureSimple, high-percentage strikes from Karate and Muay Thai, drilled from realistic starting positions.
  4. Week 04 Breaking grips & escapesReleasing wrist grabs, bear hugs, chokes and hair grabs — techniques drawn from Jiu-Jitsu.
  5. Week 05 Takedowns & ground defenceBasic Judo trips and sweeps, plus how to get back to your feet if you end up on the ground.
  6. Week 06 Putting it togetherScenario drills, stress-testing techniques under fatigue, and a personal safety plan you can keep using.

07 Questions

Before your
first class.

The things new students ask most often. If your question isn't here, message us and a coach will answer it directly.

01 Do I need any experience to start?

None at all. Every discipline at DoBu runs beginner-friendly classes, and a large proportion of our members had never trained in anything before they walked in. You'll be taught the fundamentals from scratch, and paired with experienced students during sparring so you're learning rather than just surviving.

02 What should I wear to my first class?

Loose, comfortable clothing you can move in — a t-shirt and tracksuit bottoms are ideal. Avoid anything with zips, buckles or hard seams, since you'll be in close contact with other students.

For Judo, Jiu-Jitsu and Karate we can lend you a gi for your first few classes. For Muay Thai, we have loan gloves available.

  • Bring a water bottle and a towel
  • Remove all jewellery, including rings and watches
  • Keep fingernails and toenails trimmed short
  • Arrive ten minutes early so a coach can show you around
03 Am I too old, or not fit enough?

No. Our adult classes include students from 16 to well into their 60s, and fitness is something you build through training, not a prerequisite for it. Every technique can be scaled to your current level, and you set your own pace during conditioning and sparring.

If you have an existing injury or health condition, mention it to the coach before the class starts — they'll adapt the session for you.

04 Which discipline should I start with?

It depends what you want. As a rough guide:

  • Judo — if you want a complete grappling art with throws and ground work.
  • Jiu-Jitsu — if you want the most practical ground self-defence, and prefer technique over strength.
  • Karate — if you want structure, clear progression and a traditional striking art.
  • Muay Thai — if you want serious fitness alongside effective striking.

You don't have to choose immediately — every membership includes open mat time, so you can sample more than one before deciding.

05 Is sparring compulsory?

No, never. Sparring is always optional, and beginners are never expected to take part before they're ready. Coaches will introduce it gradually and only with your agreement.

In Muay Thai especially, plenty of members train for years without sparring — they come for the fitness, the pad work and the technique.

06 How long does it take to get a black belt?

Realistically, five to eight years of consistent training, depending on the discipline and how often you train. Belt grades are earned through demonstrated skill, not time served.

More importantly, the belts are a byproduct of training, not the goal. Students who focus on turning up consistently and improving week to week progress far faster than those chasing the next grade.

07 Can I train more than one martial art?

Yes — and many members do. The Advanced membership covers any two martial arts and five sessions a week, while Elite gives unlimited access to all four disciplines.

Some pairings work particularly well: Judo and Jiu-Jitsu share a huge amount of ground work, so progress in one accelerates the other. Karate and Muay Thai both develop striking at different ranges, making them a strong combination.

08 Do you offer classes for children?

Yes. Judo, Jiu-Jitsu and Karate all run dedicated kids' classes, typically in the 15:00–17:00 slot on weekdays. Junior membership is £25 per month and covers all kids' martial arts sessions.

Kids' classes focus on fundamentals, coordination and respect on the mat, taught in a structured but game-led format. Parents are welcome to watch from the seating area.

09 What if I try it and don't like it?

Your first class is free, so there's nothing to lose. If it's not for you, that's completely fine — no pressure and no follow-up sales pitch.

If you'd rather try a different discipline before deciding, just say so and we'll book you into another trial class.
